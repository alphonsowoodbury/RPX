About

A race companion, built in the open.

RPX is a Formula 1 companion you open while watching — the schedule, the circuit, the stakes, the standings. Underneath, it's a data and AI platform. This page is the whole of it: what it is, how it's built, and the plan from here.

I'm Alphonso Woodbury — a senior software and data engineer. This is where I point that at the sport I can't stop watching: a tool I actually use on race weekends, built to be fast, clear, and genuinely useful.

The site doubles as an open record of how I build — data engineering, AI, and design, all in one place.

What it is · the thesis

Most data projects stop at a dashboard. This one keeps going — all the way to a model that learns from human judgment and an evaluation harness that tells you whether it actually got better. That full loop, from a real-time event stream to an evaluated, served model, is the shape of the work an AI lab's data org does every day. RPX builds that loop end to end, at hobby scale but on the real tools.

Two principles hold the whole thing together. Everything is real. A streaming pipeline that processes three events isn't streaming experience — so the live tier runs against an actual race weekend, with the lag, backpressure, and replay that come with it. And each layer earns its place. The cheap, elegant version of the companion still ships and stays fast; the heavy, industry-standard machinery lives behind it, where the engineering story is.

A complete vertical slice — ingest, label, train, evaluate, serve — not another chart.
F1 telemetry is a genuine high-rate stream and seventy years of structured history. The perfect domain to build the real thing on.

The build · 0 → 100

Eight phases, zero to platform.

Cross-cutting from day one: CI/CD (GitHub Actions), secrets, infra-as-code (Terraform), and a hard $0 cost line. Each phase ships on its own and stands up the next.
